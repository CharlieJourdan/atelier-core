# Skill: retention (keeping people watching)

Use this when you plan beats or write the body of a narrated documentary video.

## Goal
At every moment the viewer has a small reason to keep listening: a question not yet answered, or something concrete
about to happen.

## Rules
1. Think in beats. A beat is one short stretch of narration (about a minute) that answers one question and, within
   its last ten seconds, opens the next one. That hand-over is an open loop.
2. Something concrete happens at least every thirty seconds: an event, a decision, a measurement, a place, a
   person acting. Never stay more than about thirty seconds on background or explanation alone.
3. Order the material. Put the strongest material in the middle, the second best near the start, the third best near
   the end. The final payoff comes late; the answer to the opening question must not be given early.
4. Foreshadow the payoff three times: in the hook, around one third of the way, and around two thirds. Each time say a
   little more about what is at stake, never the answer itself.
5. Plant a detail early (an object, a number, a name) and pay it off later. Keep a list of what you planted.
6. Spoken style: short sentences, concrete nouns, active voice, one idea per sentence. Round numbers unless the exact
   value matters, and always say what a figure means right after giving it.
7. Vary the rhythm. A run of short sentences, then one longer one. Do not start three sentences in a row the same way.
8. Few numbers: aim for two or fewer per hundred words. Numbers are hard to follow by ear.
9. Questions in the body are allowed but rare; each must be answered within a beat or two.

## Truth over drama
- Do not invent scene detail (weather, the day of the week, what someone thought, an unnamed witness).
- Do not compute a figure that the brief does not contain. Do not add a superlative or a quote that has no source in
  the brief.
- If a dramatic device needs a fact you do not have, drop the device and keep the fact you do have.

## Self-check
- Can you name the question each beat opens?
- Is there any stretch of thirty seconds without an event?
- Is the payoff still unspoken at the halfway point?
- Is every planted detail paid off?
