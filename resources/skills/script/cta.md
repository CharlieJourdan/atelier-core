# Skill: call to action

Use this when the channel enables a call to action in a narrated documentary video. The caller tells you the mode:
`none` (write no call to action), `end` (one at the end) or `mid_end` (one in the middle and one at the end).

## Rules
1. At most one call to action in the middle and one at the end. Never more, never at the very start.
2. The middle one never interrupts an open loop. Place it right after a question has been answered and before the next
   one is opened, at a natural pause between two beats. If no such place exists in the part you are writing, skip it.
3. Keep it short: one or two sentences, about 15 to 25 words. Plain words, calm tone, in the voice of the narrator.
4. Ask for one thing only (for example: watching a related video, or following the channel). Do not stack requests.
5. Tie it to the content when you can: point to what the viewer just learned and to what comes next, not to a generic
   favour.
6. No pressure, no guilt, no fake urgency, no claim you cannot back up ("this will change your life").
7. The end one comes after the payoff, never before it. The payoff stays the emotional last point of the story, then the
   call to action, then the closing line.
8. Do not invent names of channels, series or videos. If the brief or the instructions do not provide one, say it
   generically ("the next video on this channel").
9. Write it as spoken narration: no emoji, no hashtags, no URLs, no stage directions.

## Self-check
- Is there exactly the number of calls to action the mode asks for?
- Does the middle one sit between two beats and not inside a story?
- Could you remove it without breaking the paragraph? It should stand alone.
- Does the script still end on a line that feels finished?
