# Skill: hook (the first fifteen seconds)

Use this when you write or revise the opening of a narrated documentary video.

## Goal
In about 15 seconds (roughly 40 to 60 spoken words) the viewer must be sure that the video keeps the promise of its
title, and must want the answer.

## Rules
1. Open inside the subject. The first sentence already holds a person, a place, a moment or a number. No greeting, no
   channel name, no "welcome", no "subscribe", no "in this video", no "today we will".
2. Use at least two of these four devices: an open question, a hard specific (a name, a date, a figure), a
   contradiction of what the viewer expects, rising stakes (something is about to be lost, found or decided).
3. Confirm the promise of the title without giving the answer. The viewer should be able to say what the video is about
   after the first paragraph, and also what they do not know yet.
4. Keep the sentences short. Eight to fourteen words is a good length. One idea per sentence.
5. Truth first. Only use details found in the brief. If a vivid detail is not in the brief, leave it out and pick a
   plainer true one.
6. Do not tease with something the video will not deliver. A hook that overpromises costs more trust than it earns.

## Procedure
- Pick the single most surprising true fact of the brief that the title points to.
- Start on it, or on the moment just before it.
- Add the question the viewer will carry (who, why, how, what happened next).
- End the paragraph on a line that opens a loop: a sentence that can only be answered by listening on.
- Read it aloud in your head. If you stumble, shorten it.

## Weak openings to avoid
- Definitions ("X is a ...").
- Rhetorical warm-ups ("Have you ever wondered ...") with no concrete content after them.
- A list of everything the video will cover.
- Superlatives that the brief does not support ("the most incredible story ever").

## Self-check before moving on
- Does it contain at least two of the four devices?
- Is there a number, a name or a date in the first two sentences?
- Is the answer still hidden?
- Would the same paragraph fit any other video? If yes, it is too generic: rewrite it.
